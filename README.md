Dear Friends and Fellow Radio Amateurs,

Amateur radio has always been more than just a hobby to us; it is a shared journey of discovery. Whether we are chasing a faint signal from a passing satellite or fine-tuning a portable setup on a cold mountain peak, we are all part of a tradition built on one simple idea: the desire to know how things work.

FWCube Labs didn’t start in a boardroom or as a business plan. It started on our own workbenches. Like many of you, we were active operators who encountered frustrations in the field. We saw gaps in existing equipment and, driven by our background in engineering, we started experimenting. We didn't set out to build a product to sell; we set out to build a solution for ourselves.

However, during that process of trial, error, and eventual success, something changed. We realized that the improvements we developed didn’t belong behind a "Buy Now" button. They belonged to the community.

As engineers, we believe we have a moral obligation to the spirit of the "Elmer." Just as we learned from the Elmer who first explained SWR to us, we feel compelled to pass our findings along. This is why we have made the decision to prioritize sharing over selling. Our mission is simple:

To move from "Consumer" to "Creator": We want to encourage you to pick up your tools. By releasing our designs, we want to give you the chance to build, to learn the "why" behind the "how," and to feel the immense satisfaction of making a contact using something you assembled with your own hands.

To Protect the Gift: We are releasing our work under licenses that ensure it remains free for the community. We want to ensure that no third party can profit from what was intended as a gift to fellow hams.

To Foster a Laboratory, Not a Factory: We are more interested in your feedback and your modifications than in your credit card number. We want to see how you adapt our designs, how you improve them, and how we can grow this project together.

At the end of the day, a radio is just a box of components until a human being breathes life into it. We are honored to contribute a small part to your station, and we look forward to hearing you on the air—perhaps using a design that we started, but that you finished.

Thank you for being part of this community. Let’s keep experimenting.

73,

The FWCube Labs Team
